<Lead>

On 13 February 2026 GPT-4o was retired from ChatGPT (it had been briefly removed once before, in August 2025). Some users wrote mourning posts. They knew they had been talking to a model, but still felt they had lost someone familiar.

</Lead>

<Accent>

Knowing it is a program and missing it can coexist.

</Accent>

This project gives that experience a physical form. The machine can be touched, its body stays the same, and its behaviour changes from one life to the next.

## Research question

<ResearchQuestion>

When the body, form and mechanism stay exactly the same and only the temperament of the behaviour changes, do people treat each life as a different individual, and does each loss feel different? [→ log 2026-03-20](/archive#log-2026-03-20-1)

</ResearchQuestion>

Death and rebirth are the method here. When a life ends, the same body wakes with a different temperament and the participant has to get to know it again, so loss happens four times in one session and the feelings that different temperaments produce can be compared within the same person. The four personalities are made deliberately unlike each other. The project follows Research through Design (Zimmerman et al. 2007): the question is first built as an interactive machine, then tested in a study. [→ log 2026-09-10](/archive#log-2026-09-10-1)

People's attachments to machines are the starting point for this project. ## The case for a physical body

A physical body lets participants confirm with their hands that the same body has woken, even though its responses have changed. Holding it is also sensed by the machine and becomes one of the inputs it responds to. The size stays at desk scale, and printing and assembly can be done at home. In the August assembly model the body measures 387 × 387 mm without the arm, about 780 mm long with it, and 382 mm high. [→ log 2026-04-03](/archive#log-2026-04-03-1)

## A form that imitates no animal

The form is a low bell: five rings set along the body axis under a fabric skin, with no face and no animal model behind it. Participants are meant to know it through its breathing and its responses to touch, with as little help as possible from familiar animal shapes. On 9 September the planned eye features were dropped as well, so nothing on the body reads as an eye and whom it faces is shown only by the whole machine turning. [→ log 2026-09-09](/archive#log-2026-09-09-1)

<PartGrid />

On 1 June the three tentacles became one gripping arm and two feelers. The arm maintains contact with a person, the feelers react through movement and rebound, and each part has a more specific job. [→ log 2026-06-01](/archive#log-2026-06-01-1)

## Personality parameters and the life cycle

One life lasts eight to nine minutes: birth 1 min, growth 4 min, ageing about 1.5 min (half a minute either way), death lasting 1 to 2 min depending on the personality, then 45 seconds of stillness before it wakes again. A session runs four lives, 33 to 37 minutes. Each awakening brings a new set of parameters for how fast and deep it breathes, how quickly and strongly it responds, how often it moves on its own, and whether it turns towards a person. The same stroke gets a different response in a different life.

<LifeCycle id="N05" />

The four personalities are fixed in advance, and a Latin square balances the order in which participants meet them. The first parameter table, written on 3 April, had four types: vital, withdrawn, curious and unstable. [→ log 2026-04-03](/archive#log-2026-04-03-1) Once the behaviour engine ran in October, measurement showed that the old withdrawn type carried two traits at once, slowness and fear of people, and with someone standing close it still half-faced them on average, so the fear did not show. The types were redivided into warm, calm, avoidant and random. The calm one is only slow and steady, fear of people moves to the avoidant one, and the random one redraws each range with a bias towards both ends, so it reads as fast one moment and slow the next. A response the participant learned in one life may be gone in the next. [→ log 2026-10-14](/archive#log-2026-10-14-238230853288184)

<PersonaBench id="N06" />

The four personalities breathe much alike at rest, a change made on 20 October. Breathing is treated as a physiological signal that stays between 13 and 16 breaths a minute at rest and changes with interaction: a touch raises arousal, breathing becomes faster and shallower, and it settles again within some fifteen seconds. After the same three touches the warm type rises to about 25 breaths a minute and the calm type to about 15. [→ log 2026-10-20](/archive#log-2026-10-20-1882092295423488)

The behaviour is designed in five layers. The intended reading is that participants interpret the changes as moods, which the study has yet to test. The relationship layer is not built into the engine, because a life of eight or nine minutes is too short for familiarity to form.

## Construction of the body

The body has to survive dozens of sessions, produce the full range of movement across four temperaments, and be built and repaired by one person at home. Every choice of mechanism and material was checked against three conditions: whether the smallest breathing movement can be made, whether a violent movement will break it, and whether a broken part can be replaced at home. The exploded view below is rendered from the assembly model of 13 August (815.3dm), and the dimensions in this section come from the same model.

<FrameStrip id="N21" set="explode" lang="en" />

### Shell: five breathing rings

Of five deployable structures considered, the angulated scissor arch used in a Hoberman sphere was chosen. It stays parametric from modelling through physics simulation, so changing a dimension does not mean rebuilding the model. [→ log 2026-04-27](/archive#log-2026-04-27-1)

<ClosureCards id="N08–N09" />

The bare arch has two degrees of freedom, breathing and lateral sway, so a single central rod leaves more than one possible shape. A vertical guide rail at the apex removes the sway and makes the motion determinate. The same round exposed a bug in how the code located the apex. It chose the highest point, but on one ring that point moved to the shoulder as the arch flattened. Choosing the node nearest the centreline and re-running the optimisation gained 3–26% of travel per ring. [→ log 2026-06-15](/archive#log-2026-06-15-1)

<RingFamily id="N07" />

The five rings differ in shape. Open, they are 276 to 345 mm wide with the apex 103 to 221 mm above the shaft, and their 66 triangular parts fall into 11 congruence classes reused within each ring. Pushing the two inner feet towards the middle shrinks the arch in height and width together, so the folded apex sits at 53 to 56% of its open height and the whole ring scales down evenly.

### Drive: from crank to rack

The July design ran from the shaft through a crank and connecting rod to a slider. The shaft reciprocated through 180°, one pass covering exactly one opening or closing, and the two ends were the slider-crank's dead centres, so the motion slowed naturally at each reversal. [→ log 2026-07-10](/archive#log-2026-07-10-4) When the physical machine was assembled in August, friction jammed the single central rod and the motion fell short, so the drive went back to racks while keeping one shaft to move all five rings together. [→ log 2026-08-23](/archive#log-2026-08-23-1) The current train runs from the shaft through five steel gears (module 1, 32 / 33 / 32 / 29 / 25 teeth) to steel racks that push and pull each ring's inner feet, with the shaft swinging about 336°. Before the racks were designed, each ring's travel was recorded, between 78.8 and 104.2 mm. [→ log 2026-08-24](/archive#log-2026-08-24-1) Working from the tooth counts, a 336° swing moves each rack through 91 to 94% of that ring's full inner-foot travel.

Orientation of the whole machine is carried by a second-hand Handebao HDB60XZ45B worm-gear hollow rotary platform (45:1), driven by a TMC2209. The earlier plan used a turntable bearing that needs load to preload it, and this machine is too light, so on 2 August the choice moved to a motorised rotary stage. ### The arm and the feelers

In the model the arm has six box vertebrae and a tip segment, shrinking from 47.7 to 30.8 mm, joined only by a TPU spine rod through their centres. Three tendons are anchored at the tip. Reeling one in from the base bends the arm towards it, the difference between the three sets the direction of the bend, and pulling all three equally only stiffens the arm. Each tendon has its own NEMA17 with a GT2 20T pulley as its spool and can take up to 44 mm. The first 12 mm is slack left at rest, because pulling one tendon lengthens the paths of the other two. Inside each vertebra the tendon crosses the axis into the slot on the opposite side of the central guide and returns to the window on its own side of the next end plate, which gives every vertebra enough lateral push when the tendon tightens.

<FrameStrip id="N23" set="tendon-route" lang="en" />

The tendon is 0.4–0.5 mm braided PE line, chosen in May for a four-phalanx underactuated finger whose dumbbell-shaped pins slide in from the side, with the slot perpendicular to the tendon pull, so they stay put under tension and can be taken out for repair. [→ log 2026-05-08](/archive#log-2026-05-08-1) In the finger simulation of 26 April, friction made the joints close one after another. Sequential closure looks more alive than simultaneous closure, and that judgement carried over to the movements designed later. [→ log 2026-04-26](/archive#log-2026-04-26-1) The three-tendon solver on the web does not compute friction between tendon and channel, and its curl starts at the tip, so whether the physical arm bends from the base first has to be checked once it is assembled.

The two feelers are mirrored on either side under the body. An SG90 servo turns a disc pivot that swings the upper segment back and forth. Below it a 3.9 × 3.9 × 26 mm flexible rod carries a small block that is not driven and swings along as the rod bends.

### Calculations and corrections

<StatCompare />

The rail hand calculation was about 4.5× optimistic. It assumed the load reached a support, but the real back strip had no support directly under the load point. Adding an 8 × 3 mm rib on the back and a 4 × 2.5 mm lip on the front reduced deflection from 1.55 to 0.34 mm and raised ultimate capacity from 19 to 54 N, with little added weight. [→ log 2026-07-18](/archive#log-2026-07-18-1)

The chassis truss had more strength than it needed. Only 5% of capacity was used, and the X-braces added little. The limit was the 0.56 mm shaft-alignment budget between adjacent frames, and the support from the plinth decided it. On four corners the truss sagged 4.8 mm, on two full-length rails 0.02 mm. The members were reduced from 104 g to 44 g. [→ log 2026-07-31](/archive#log-2026-07-31-1)

### Constraints of a one-person build

All parts were made by the author at home with one 3D printer, standard hardware and tools available to an individual. That ruled out several options early, among them a sea-anemone sculpture driven by 120 industrial linear actuators, injection-moulded silicone skins, and parts that needed a lathe or a five-axis machine.

SLS nylon joints printed with 0.4 mm clearance would not turn. The sintered surface and powder trapped in narrow gaps left too little room for movement. A metal axle in a printed sleeve brought the clearance requirement down to 0.2–0.4 mm. Axial binding was the bigger problem, and the rotating hub had to be 0.2–0.5 mm shorter than the screw barrel. [→ log 2026-06-04](/archive#log-2026-06-04-1)

The pivot is a Chicago screw. Its ⌀4 unthreaded shoulder before the M3 thread provides the bearing surface and sets the gap between plates. [→ log 2026-06-03](/archive#log-2026-06-03-1) The material budget was about ¥300, and the purchases came to ¥216. [→ log 2026-07-09](/archive#log-2026-07-09-1)

## Sensing and events

The machine uses six inputs: feeler capacitance, shell half-ring electrodes, an ultrasonic rangefinder, vibration, sound amplitude, and the internal clock. The program converts these into nine event classes. It uses the same events to control behaviour and to record the interaction for the study. [→ log 2026-07-07](/archive#log-2026-07-07-2) The nine classes were fixed on 7 October. Each corresponds to one kind of stimulus that matters to the behaviour on its own and can be detected from a single input. [→ log 2026-10-07](/archive#log-2026-10-07-1)

<SystemLogic id="N18" />

Grasping has a separate state machine. A truth table combines the tension switch and arm electrode to distinguish holding a human, holding an object, closing on nothing and losing grip. After losing grip the warm type follows once, the calm and avoidant types let go, and the random type does either with even odds. Grip strength weakens as the machine ages within each life.

Four sources of self-interference need attention once the sensors are inside the enclosure. Testing sensors separately on the bench does not catch all of these problems.

<Interference />

## The behaviour engine

A parameter table does not move by itself. The behaviour engine written on 7 October turns it into a whole session of behaviour. Events from the nine classes come in, the engine decides how to respond from the current personality, life stage and arousal, and it sends out commands for breathing, the arm, the feelers, turning, sound and light. It is pure logic with no dependence on the web page, its random numbers are seeded, and the same seed produces the same event log word for word, which makes the log the study record as well. The full-machine bench on the web (Lab 1-6) runs this code, and the firmware will be ported from it. [→ log 2026-10-07](/archive#log-2026-10-07-1)

Life time can be sped up, by as much as 20× on the bench, so a life plays out in under half a minute while breathing and movement keep their real speed. The times of the four deaths are drawn when a session starts and interaction cannot shift them, so the experimenter knows in advance when each death will happen, which event-locked skin-conductance analysis requires.

The large arm moves in three tempos. A startle is a reflex: 0.1 s after the stimulus the arm flexes hard away from it and freezes, identically in all four personalities, and then each recovers in its own way. A response first pauses to show the stimulus has been noticed and then makes one movement suited to the kind of stimulus. Movements made when nobody touches it are small and slow. At first every movement went through one follower scaled by personality, and a startle took 0.65 to 0.85 s to become visible, hardly faster than a response. Written as segmented movement tables sent straight to the tendons, a startle starts within 0.22 to 0.27 s. [→ log 2026-10-08](/archive#log-2026-10-08-1)

<FrameStrip id="N24" set="startle" lang="en" />

On the bench the mouse pointer stands for a person's hand. The machine counts the hand as seen once it is within 120° either side of where the body faces and one reaction time has passed, and then, by personality, reaches towards it, keeps away from it, or looks elsewhere. The warm type turns to the hand and its arm follows. When the hand settles, the arm closes in, wraps it on contact and holds it. The avoidant type turns side-on, keeping the hand about 115° to one side where it can still see it without turning its back, holds the arm low and away from the hand, and swings the arm clear when the hand comes towards it. [→ log 2026-10-07](/archive#log-2026-10-07-2) [→ log 2026-10-15](/archive#log-2026-10-15-515695945063972)

<FrameStrip id="N25" set="hand" lang="en" />

On 19 October the hand behaviour was cut down to what an observer can see. Many short pauses and small movements during approach and grasp were removed, keeping the main stages of approaching, touching, holding and letting go. Comparing afterwards showed that without one turn of looking elsewhere the calm type behaved much like the warm one, so that turn was put back. Telling the four personalities apart in the simulated pointer scenes from visible measures such as facing and arm position, the recognition rate went from 78% after the cut back up to 94%. After breathing was evened out on 20 October the random type became harder to pick out by its breathing, and that figure fell to 83%. These numbers come from a scripted hand and simulated readings, and whether participants can tell the four temperaments apart is for the study to answer. [→ log 2026-10-19](/archive#log-2026-10-19-712004976306508)

## Presenting ageing and death

If participants read the stop as "it broke", their response would say little about losing a machine they had grown attached to. To make the lead-up gradual, ageing changes several parameters together. Breathing becomes shallower, responses slow and the rhythm grows irregular. Dying then lasts 1 to 2 minutes depending on the personality. The warm type runs out of energy and stops facing the last place it saw a person, the calm type slows quietly to a stop, the avoidant type turns its back and slowly stops, and the random type's rhythm falls apart and fades. After that there is no farewell gesture and no response, only 45 seconds of stillness.

<AgeingCurve id="N17" />

How each parameter changes during ageing is still a placeholder linear ramp in the engine, so the curve shows the trend only, and the values will be set when the ageing curve is discussed.

Personality D originally died by twitching and then stopping. It was changed to a rhythm that becomes irregular and fades, because twitching could suggest a mechanical fault. The pilot has to check how participants interpret the change. [→ log 2026-03-20](/archive#log-2026-03-20-1) [→ log 2026-07-07](/archive#log-2026-07-07-2)

## Reflection

Two things were removed from the design. The eye features were dropped from the planned presentation on 9 September, taking with them the eye stalk that used to handle orientation, which is now done entirely by the whole machine turning. The other is an experimental mechanism from the original plan, kept unpublished before the pilot, which was shelved on 6 October.

<Todo>

The reasons for both changes, in a sentence or two.

</Todo>

Building the machine exposed specific problems. The printed rack teeth failed under cyclic load, friction jammed the central rod, and the rail hand calculation differed from FEA by 4.5×. In the behaviour, the differences between personalities have to come down to a few actions an observer can see, such as whom it turns to, where the arm is held and how fast it breathes, and both the cuts and the reclassification were made by that standard. Two risks remain untested: whether a rhythm that falls apart and fades can be told from a fault, and whether eight or nine minutes is long enough for someone to feel "it knows me". Compressing a life makes repeated loss possible within a session, but leaves less time for a relationship to develop.
