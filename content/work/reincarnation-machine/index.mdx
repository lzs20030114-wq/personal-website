On 13 February 2026 GPT-4o was retired from ChatGPT (it had been briefly removed once before, in August 2025). People mourned a model version, and the surprising part was not that they grieved. It was that **they all knew it was a model.** They understood perfectly well what was on the other side, and still lost something specific.

I wanted to pull this out of the screen and make it a physical process you can put your hands on. Same mechanism, except now the loss happens on your desk, at a speed you can watch.

<ProcessAside>
**Where it sits.** The established fact is that people form real attachments to machines and grieve when they "die." That isn't what I'm trying to prove. It's where I start. **Heider & Simmel (1944)** — self-moving geometric shapes are enough to trigger attributions of life. **Kate Darling's Pleo studies** — an hour of interaction is enough that people refuse to "hurt" a toy robot. **AIBO funerals in Japan** — people hold actual funerals for a discontinued robot dog. **Tamagotchi** — death and reset, on a screen, with no body. **_Can't Help Myself_ (Sun Yuan & Peng Yu, 2016)** — machine death as spectacle, but with no personality and no rebirth. **Ian Cheng's _BOB_** — a personality that evolves, but on a screen, and it does not die. **The empty cell**: physical embodiment, personality, emotional bond, death, and cyclical rebirth in one mechanism.
</ProcessAside>

## Why it has to be physical

A tabletop object roughly 33 × 34 × 22 cm when fully expanded, dropping to about 15.5 cm tall as it contracts. It is fixed to the desk and cannot move around — everything it expresses, it expresses in place, through breathing, posture and where it points. Holding it is also one of the channels by which it senses you. [→ log 2026-04-03](/archive#log-2026-04-03-1)

## Why it does not look like an animal

It has no face and imitates no known animal. That is deliberate. If it looked like a pet, the empathy would be bought by the shape, and behaviour would stop being the only variable. **It should look like a machine and feel like it is alive.**

- **The breathing shell.** Five expanding rings arranged along the body into a low ridge, opening and closing together. This is the primary carrier of aliveness — rate, depth and regularity of breathing hold most of the information about personality and about ageing.
- **One tendon-driven arm.** A segmented finger pulled by braided line; it carries response strength and voluntary reaching.
- **Two compliant feelers.** A springy core inside a silicone sleeve, one micro servo at the base each. Their whip and rebound come from the material, not from code. They double as touch electrodes.
- **The base.** Motors, controller and power live here, keeping the mass at the bottom.

Three tentacles became one gripping arm and two reacting feelers for the same reason: the arm carries the relationship, the feelers carry reaction, everything else is noise. [→ log 2026-06-01](/archive#log-2026-06-01-1)

## Personality is twelve numbers

The hardware is identical in every life. What changes between one life and the next is a table of numbers — which is what makes the concept and the experiment the same object: form is the constant, personality is the only variable.

One full life is compressed into roughly eight minutes — birth 1 min, growth and interaction 4 min, ageing 1.5 min, death, then 45 seconds of blank — and a session runs four of them, about 37 minutes. Then a new life wakes up: different rhythm, a completely different answer to the same touch. Everything you learned about getting along with the last one is void.

**Five layers of aliveness.** ① **Self-motion** — it moves whether or not you touch it; geometric shapes moving on their own are enough to trigger attributions of life. ② **Non-deterministic response** — touch produces a response, but not the same one every time, which blocks the button-and-reaction model. ③ **Internal state** — the same touch gets different answers at different moments, so people start explaining behaviour with feelings; this is where each life's personality lives. ④ **Relationship** — over time it answers your habitual touch more smoothly. In eight minutes this layer barely exists; that is the price of the accelerated life cycle, see the reflection. ⑤ **Initiative** — when you are away its behaviour shifts in a way that points at you, subtly enough that you narrate it yourself. Death lands because all five go to zero at once.

## How the body was built

The concept puts three demands on the body: one shell has to survive dozens of sessions; four temperaments from the mildest to the most violent all have to be producible; and it has to be built by one person at home. Every decision below follows from those three, and every failure is one of them sending its bill.

### The shell: five breathing rings

Of five deployable structures I picked the angulated scissor arch (the mechanism inside a Hoberman sphere), for a very practical reason: it is the only one that stays parametric all the way from modelling to physics simulation, so changing one dimension does not mean starting over. [→ log 2026-04-27](/archive#log-2026-04-27-1)

The closure condition is Σ(180−β)=180. The bare arch has **two degrees of freedom** — breathing, plus lateral sway — so a single central rod leaves the shape ambiguous at any given moment; a vertical guide rail at the apex kills the sway and the motion becomes determinate. The same round flushed out a bug: the code located the apex by taking the highest point, but one ring flattens and its highest point migrates out to the shoulder — switching to "the node nearest the centreline" and re-running the optimisation gained 3–26% of travel per ring. [→ log 2026-06-15](/archive#log-2026-06-15-1)

### The arm and the feelers

The arm is four tapered phalanges (25 / 20 / 15 / 10 mm). Dumbbell-shaped pins slide in from the side with the slot perpendicular to the tendon pull: no press fit, nothing to pull out, and the finger comes apart for repair at any time. The tendon is 0.4–0.5 mm braided line running through 1.0–1.2 mm channels inside the printed parts. [→ log 2026-05-08](/archive#log-2026-05-08-1)

The April simulation left one finding behind: friction makes the joints close one after another rather than all at once — a delay that should not be designed out, because it is exactly why the motion reads as alive. It later became a principle for the whole machine. [→ log 2026-04-26](/archive#log-2026-04-26-1)

The springy core inside each feeler is doing three jobs at once: it is the skeleton setting bending stiffness and rebound, the posture coupling a single base servo grips to aim it, and a capacitive touch electrode, so one wire makes the entire length touch-sensitive. One expensive trap: buy **superelastic** nitinol, which springs at room temperature — not **shape-memory** wire, which only contracts when heated. They look identical. [To confirm: how many tendons the physical arm has. The simulation settled on three, and the purchase list buys one tensioner microswitch per tendon on that basis, but whether the three are independently driven was never adjudicated in the record.]

### What was computed, and what was computed wrong

The hand calculation was about **4.5× optimistic**: rail deflection 1.55 → 0.34 mm, ultimate capacity 19 → 54 N. Hand formulas assume the load reaches a support, and in the real part the back strip has none directly under the load point; an 8 × 3 mm rib on the back and a 4 × 2.5 mm lip on the front buy it back at almost no weight cost. [→ log 2026-07-18](/archive#log-2026-07-18-1) The chassis truss was wrong in the other direction: strength uses only 5% of capacity and the X-braces are decorative, while the real limit is the 0.56 mm shaft-alignment budget between adjacent frames — and that is decided by how the plinth supports it. On four corners it sags 4.8 mm; on two full-length rails, 0.02 mm. The members went from 104 g to 44 g. [→ log 2026-07-31](/archive#log-2026-07-31-1)

### Built solo

No team, no workshop, no industrial processes. Every part came off one 3D printer, plus tools and standard hardware a single person can buy. This constraint isn't romanticism, it's a design input: it eliminated whole categories of solution up front — the sea-anemone sculpture driven by 120 industrial linear actuators, injection-moulded silicone skins, anything needing a lathe or a five-axis machine.

Why printed joints seize: SLS nylon printed with 0.4 mm clearance came out dead, because SLS parts carry a sintered skin and trap powder in narrow gaps; a metal axle in a printed sleeve brings the requirement down to 0.2–0.4 mm. The more useful lesson is that axial binding kills before radial does — the rotating hub must be 0.2–0.5 mm **shorter** than the screw barrel. [→ log 2026-06-04](/archive#log-2026-06-04-1) The pivot is a Chicago screw: the ⌀4 unthreaded shoulder before the M3 thread is both the bearing surface and the spacer that sets the gap between plates. [→ log 2026-06-03](/archive#log-2026-06-03-1) Material budget about ¥300; ¥216 actually spent. [→ log 2026-07-09](/archive#log-2026-07-09-1)

## Making the stop read as death

The most likely failure of this machine is not mechanical, it is the legibility of death: if the stop reads as "it broke," every grief measure goes to zero. So ageing is not a separate state — it is every parameter drifting at once: breathing shallows, latency stretches, the rhythm turns irregular. Death is quiet: no farewell gesture, no response after the last breath, then 45 seconds of blank.

Personality D originally died by twitching and then stopping. It was changed to a rhythm that falls apart and fades, for a very specific reason: twitching is exactly what a broken machine looks like, and it would actively invite a fault attribution instead of a death attribution. [→ log 2026-03-20](/archive#log-2026-03-20-1) [→ log 2026-07-07](/archive#log-2026-07-07-2)

## How it senses you

Six inputs: feeler capacitance (MPR121 ch0–3), shell half-ring electrodes (ch4–5), an ultrasonic rangefinder, vibration (MPU6050, envelope only, no attitude), sound amplitude, and the internal clock. They are translated into a nine-class event vocabulary, and the same event stream both drives the behaviour engine and is the experimental record — the device annotates itself. [→ log 2026-07-07](/archive#log-2026-07-07-2)

Grasping has its own state machine: a truth table over the tension switch and the arm electrode separates holding a human / holding an object / closed on nothing / lost grip. Grip strength weakens with each ageing cycle.

[To come: the full list of the nine event classes. Only ARM_TOUCH, RESISTANCE and the four grasp states are written down anywhere, while the number nine is itself frozen — the missing classes have to come from the author.]

Four self-interference problems had to be designed out — all of them the kind that works on the bench and fails inside the enclosure: motor electrical noise reaching the accelerometer, speaker vibration feeding back into the microphone, ultrasonic readings collapsing when a hand rests on the shell, and the capacitive baseline tracker swallowing a long press as environmental drift (anything held over ten seconds gets falsely released).

| Cycle | Behaviour | What they will report | Physiology |
|---|---|---|---|
| Life 1 + death 1 | Intimacy builds fast — frequent touching, an early name — and strong holding and speech at the death | Sharp drop in self-rated mood, distress 70–80 of 100, strong separation anxiety | The largest skin-conductance response of the session; heart rate clearly up |

**Fallback.** If the behaviour engine isn't convincing by the pilot, switch to Wizard-of-Oz — the experimenter triggers behaviour from behind the scenes, disclosed honestly in the paper. This is standard practice in HRI, and writing it into the risk plan means the behaviour engine stops being a single point of failure.

## Reflection

**Two things cut.** The first was hidden complicity: your touching secretly shortening its life, disclosed at debriefing. [To come: why it was cut — ethical cost, implementation cost, or both; and whether it is finally cut at all.] The second was the eye stalk, an organ that did nothing but point, whose job is now carried by the whole-machine yaw platform. [To come: why it was cut.] Both are in the log, and they stay here because having considered something is not the same as never having thought of it.

**What only became clear by building it.** Twitching reads as a fault. Printed rack teeth do not survive cyclic load. Hand calculations and FEA differ by 4.5×. And in eight minutes the "it knows me" layer barely exists — the accelerated life cycle buys a repeatable loss at the price of a relationship that never has time to accumulate.

**Doing it again.** [To come: a sentence or two from the author.] With more resources I'd build the two-device version, where the relationship between two machines is visible to the user and the survivor degrades after the other one dies. That's where the genuinely new question is.
