# 非项目对话｜How compound interest works

### 用户

> Show me how compound interest works

### 助手

> Here's an interactive explorer for compound interest — drag any slider to see the effect in real time.
>
> The core idea is simple: you earn interest not just on your original money, but on all the interest you've already earned. This creates a snowball effect — the longer the time horizon, the faster the gap between "what you put in" (blue dashed line) and "what you have" (green area) grows.
>
> A few things worth noticing:
>
> **Time is the biggest lever.** Doubling your return rate helps, but doubling your time horizon has a far more dramatic effect. This is why starting early matters so much — even small contributions in your 20s compound into large sums by retirement.
>
> **The "hockey stick" shape** is the visual signature of compounding. Growth looks slow and boring for years, then suddenly accelerates. Most of the gains come in the final third of the timeline.
>
> **Monthly contributions supercharge it.** Try setting contributions to $0 vs $500 — the difference over 30 years is enormous, because each new contribution also starts compounding immediately.
>
> The formula behind it: **A = P(1 + r/n)^(nt)**, where P is principal, r is annual rate, n is compounding frequency, and t is years. The widget compounds monthly, which is typical for investment accounts.
